Disability Resources in the Capital Region

A local starting list for families in Albany, Saratoga, Schenectady, Rensselaer, and the surrounding counties. These are public programs and established nonprofits; Spectrum Arch isn't affiliated with them.

Last reviewed November 10, 2026

OPWDD in the Capital District

The Capital District region of OPWDD covers Albany, Fulton, Montgomery, Rensselaer, Saratoga, Schenectady, Schoharie, Warren, and Washington counties. Columbia and Greene are part of OPWDD's Taconic region. All of them are within OPWDD Region 3.

  • The Front Door is where you start if you think someone may be eligible for OPWDD services. Capital District Front Door: 518-388-0398. The Front Door connects you with a Care Coordination Organization, which assembles the eligibility application.
  • Self-Direction questions for Region 3 can go to the regional Self-Direction mailbox, SPS.R3.SD@opwdd.ny.gov.
  • OPWDD's regional offices are on the O.D. Heck campus, 500 Balltown Road, Schenectady.

New to OPWDD? Read how to apply for eligibility and how to choose a CCO.

Autism evaluation and training

  • Center for Autism and Related Disabilities (CARD) at the University at Albany, 1535 Western Avenue, Albany. CARD provides comprehensive autism evaluations, and training and consultation for families and professionals. albany.edu/autism

Independent living centers

Independent living centers are run by and for people with disabilities. They help with benefits, housing, advocacy, peer support, and living independently. Centers serving the Capital Region include:

  • Independent Living Center of the Hudson Valley, Troy
  • Capital District Center for Independence, Albany
  • Southern Adirondack Independent Living (SAIL), Glens Falls, with an office in Saratoga Springs

Service areas overlap, so check the state's independent living center directory for the center that covers your county.

Support from other parents

  • Parent to Parent of New York State matches parents of children and adults with disabilities with trained parent volunteers who've been through something similar. Read more in our post on caregiver support.
  • Autism Society of the Greater Capital Region, a local Autism Society affiliate, offers support and family events.

Young children and school

  • Early Intervention (birth to age 3) is run through each county's Early Intervention program. See Early Intervention in New York.
  • Preschool and school-age special education go through your school district's CPSE and CSE. See CPSE and preparing for a CSE meeting.

Getting around

  • CDTA STAR paratransit serves people who can't use fixed-route buses in parts of Albany, Rensselaer, Saratoga, and Schenectady counties. See our guide to STAR.
  • Medicaid transportation covers rides to medical appointments for people on Medicaid. See how it works.

Work and benefits

  • ACCES-VR, the state's vocational rehabilitation agency, helps people with disabilities prepare for and find work, including students in transition. acces.nysed.gov/vr
  • For SSI, Medicaid, and work incentives, see SSI at 18 and Ticket to Work.

Crisis and emergency help

In an emergency, call 911. For a mental health crisis, call or text 988. For people with developmental disabilities, see crisis services in New York.

Self-Direction and Support Brokerage

If your family member is eligible for OPWDD services and wants more control over their supports, Self-Direction may be a good fit. Spectrum Arch connects Capital Region families with OPWDD-authorized Support Brokers and offers free consultations. Contact us anytime.

Sources: OPWDD, Support Broker Authorization FAQ (regional contacts), July 2026; OPWDD, CARD location listing; NYSED ACCES-VR, Independent Living Center listing. Contact details change; confirm with each organization.
