OPWDD's CAS and CANS Information Sessions: The Capital Region Date Is November 12

By Asad M. Butt, President, Spectrum Arch, Inc. · November 10, 2026 · 2 min read

CASCANS-NYAssessmentsCapital Region

If someone in your family receives OPWDD services, they've probably had, or will soon have, a CAS assessment. Most families sit through it without really knowing what it's for. OPWDD is now running a series of information sessions called "Why the CAS/CANS Assessments Matter", and one of them is here in the Capital Region this week.

The dates

  • Capital Region (in person): Thursday, November 12, 1:30 to 3:00 pm, at the O.D. Heck campus, 500 Balltown Road, Building 3, Meeting Room 2, Schenectady.
  • Statewide webinar (evening): Wednesday, November 18, 5:00 to 6:30 pm. A good option if you work during the day.
  • A daytime statewide webinar was scheduled for November 10.

OPWDD asks people to register in advance, and says space at the in-person sessions is limited. Webinar registrants are emailed a link closer to the date. Registration is through the link on OPWDD's announcement.

What the CAS is, in plain terms

The Coordinated Assessment System (CAS) is the assessment OPWDD uses for everyone 18 or older who receives or applies for its services. A trained assessor talks with the person, and with family or others who know them well if the person wants that, and reviews documents like medical evaluations. The result is a picture of the person's strengths, needs, and interests.

Children are assessed with a different tool, the CANS-NY (Child and Adolescent Needs and Strengths).

Why it matters more than people think

A summary of the CAS is added to the person's OPWDD record, and the Care Manager is expected to go over it with the person and their planning team within 30 days. OPWDD uses CAS information to set "service ranges" that guide planning and service authorization. In other words, the assessment feeds into what services look like, which is why an assessment that undersells someone's needs can cause problems later.

Routine reassessments happen every two years, or sooner if there's a significant change in the person's condition.

How to get ready for a CAS

  • Describe a typical day honestly, including the hard parts. Families often describe a good day out of habit, or pride. The assessor needs to hear what support actually looks like.
  • Have someone there who knows the person well, if the person agrees. A parent, sibling, or long-time staff member can fill in details.
  • Bring or point to recent records: medical evaluations, behavior plans, school or program reports.
  • Ask your Care Manager for the CAS Summary once it's done, and read it. If something is wrong or missing, raise it.

If you disagree with the results

Start by talking it through with your Care Manager. If questions remain, OPWDD has a CAS/CANS Request for Review form, and you can call OPWDD at 518-473-7484. Our guide to the CAS assessment covers the whole process.

Sources: OPWDD, CAS/CANS Information Sessions (October 9, 2026); OPWDD, Reminder: CAS/CANS Information Sessions (October 22, 2026); OPWDD, Coordinated Assessment System (CAS).
