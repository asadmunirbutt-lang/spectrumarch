H.R. 1 and Medicaid: What New York Families Using OPWDD Services Should Know

By Asad M. Butt, President, Spectrum Arch, Inc. · November 10, 2026 · 2 min read

MedicaidH.R. 1BenefitsOPWDD

Almost every OPWDD service, from day programs to Self-Direction, is paid for through Medicaid. So when Medicaid rules change, families pay attention. On October 30, OPWDD's Commissioner sent a message about H.R. 1, the 2025 federal law that makes major changes to Medicaid. Here's what we know, and what we don't yet.

The two changes families are asking about

1. Work reporting requirements

Starting January 1, 2027, some New York adults ages 19 to 64 will need to show they are working, in school, in job training, or volunteering to keep Medicaid. This applies to people covered through Medicaid expansion (adults who qualify mainly based on income), not to everyone on Medicaid.

2. Renewals every six months

For that same group of adults, Medicaid will have to be renewed every six months instead of once a year.

Is my family member affected?

OPWDD's message says that "many people receiving OPWDD services are exempt from the new federal work requirements," and that some family caregivers are exempt too. The exemptions described so far include people with disabilities or who are medically frail, SSI recipients, and caregivers of young children or of someone with a disability.

"Many" isn't "all", though. Whether someone is affected depends on how they qualify for Medicaid, which isn't always obvious. An adult who gets Medicaid through SSI is in a different group from one who qualifies on income alone. And state rules on details like the definition of "medically frail" are still being worked out.

What to do now

  1. Ask your Care Manager which Medicaid group your family member is in, and whether the new rules apply. Care Managers are expected to help people keep their Medicaid.
  2. Watch the mail. Renewal and notice letters are easy to miss, and a missed renewal is still the most common way coverage lapses.
  3. If an exemption applies, be ready to show it. OPWDD advises families to be prepared to meet the conditions or show proof of an exception at renewal.
  4. Check the state's "Stay Covered" information from NY State of Health, which OPWDD points families to, including its FAQ.

For background on how adults with developmental disabilities get Medicaid in the first place, see Medicaid and OPWDD services.

We'll update this post as New York publishes more detail. If you're not sure where your family stands, contact us for a free consultation.

Sources: OPWDD, HR 1 & Upcoming Changes to Medicaid Coverage (October 30, 2026); NY State of Health, Implementing H.R. 1 Tool Kit; New York State Bar Association, H.R. 1's Changes for New York's Health Care System.
