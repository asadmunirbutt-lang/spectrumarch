When Staff Call Out: Backup Plans for Self-Directing Families

By Asad M. Butt, President, Spectrum Arch, Inc. · October 21, 2026 · 1 min read

Self-DirectionStaffingPlanning

For many families who self-direct, staffing is the hardest part. Often it isn't the hiring. It's the 6:30 a.m. text that says, "Sorry, I can't make it today."

You can't prevent every absence. You can decide ahead of time what happens when one comes.

Have more than one person

If a single staff member covers all of the hours, every absence leaves a gap. Where the budget allows, split the hours between two or three people, even if one of them only works a shift or two a week. They learn the routines, and they're the first call when someone is out.

Bringing on backup staff takes time: paperwork, background checks, and trainings through your Fiscal Intermediary. Do it before you need them, not the week someone quits.

Know your fallback options

  • Family or friends who can cover a few hours in an emergency, and who have agreed to it in advance.
  • Respite through a provider, if it's in the plan.
  • Adjusting the day. Sometimes the right answer is a quieter day at home rather than a scramble to keep every activity.

Write a one-page call-out plan

Put it on the fridge and share it with every staff member:

  1. How staff should tell you they can't come, and how much notice you expect.
  2. Who to call next, in order, with phone numbers.
  3. What the person's day looks like if plans change, and how to explain the change to them.

Changes in routine are hard for many autistic adults. A plan that includes how to tell them matters as much as who covers the shift.

Talk it through with your Support Broker

A Support Broker can help you think through staffing patterns, recruiting, and how backup coverage fits in the self-direction budget. It's worth raising at every plan review, not only after a bad week.
