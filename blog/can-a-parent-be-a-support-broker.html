Can a Parent Be a Support Broker in New York?

By Asad M. Butt, President, Spectrum Arch, Inc. · November 10, 2026 · 2 min read

Support BrokersParentsSelf-Direction

It's one of the most common questions we hear from parents in the Capital Region: "I already do all of this. Can I just be the broker?" The answer is yes, with an important limit.

Who can't be paid

OPWDD's rules say these family members cannot be paid to provide Support Broker services to their family member:

  • Parents and legal guardians
  • Spouses
  • Adult children, including sons- and daughters-in-law
  • Anyone who lives in the same household as the person who self-directs

Serving as an unpaid broker

Family members, and others, can provide Support Broker services unpaid. They must first complete the same initial training that paid brokers take: three prerecorded courses, four live Broker Training Institute modules, and the live course on building a Self-Direction budget. They don't need to apply for authorization. Instead, they use "99999" as the Support Broker authorization number on paperwork.

Should you do it?

Some parents do it well, and it can work. Before deciding, it's worth being honest about a few things:

  • Time. The training is several sessions, and the work continues all year: building the budget, amendments, renewals, staff issues.
  • The budget itself. Self-Direction budgets are built in a detailed spreadsheet that needs the desktop version of Microsoft Excel. The details matter, and mistakes can delay approval.
  • Independence. One goal of Self-Direction is a life that isn't built only around the family. A broker from outside the family can bring in new ideas and connections, and keeps going if a parent gets sick.
  • The future. If you're the broker, think about who takes over when you can't.

There's no single right answer. A middle path many families choose: a paid broker builds and maintains the budget, while the parent stays closely involved in the Circle of Support.

If you'd like to talk through what fits your family, contact us. The conversation is free. For the full picture of the broker's role, see what a Support Broker does.

Source: OPWDD, Support Broker Authorization FAQ (July 10, 2026).
