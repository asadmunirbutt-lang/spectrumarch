How to Check That a Support Broker Is Authorized by OPWDD

By Asad M. Butt, President, Spectrum Arch, Inc. · November 10, 2026 · 2 min read

Support BrokersAuthorizationSelf-Direction

When you choose a Support Broker, you're trusting someone with your family member's plan and budget. It's reasonable, and common, to ask whether they're authorized. Here's what that means and how to ask about it without it feeling awkward.

What "authorized" means

To be paid as a Support Broker in New York, a person must be authorized by OPWDD. Getting there takes real work. According to OPWDD's July 2026 Support Broker Authorization FAQ, a prospective broker must:

  • Complete eight training courses: three prerecorded prerequisites (Introduction to Self-Direction, Self-Advocacy/Self-Determination, and Person-Centered Planning), four live Broker Training Institute modules, and a live course on Developing a Self-Direction Budget. Each has a quiz with a 70% passing score.
  • Review and attest to six more required trainings, including privacy of health information, Medicaid compliance, and incident reporting.
  • Submit an authorization form to OPWDD, which processes approvals every two weeks and issues an authorization number.

It has to be renewed every year

Authorization runs until the following July 31. Every broker must reauthorize by that date each year, which includes confirming three annual trainings and logging 12 hours of professional development. A broker who misses the deadline can't bill for Support Broker services until they're reauthorized and back on OPWDD's biweekly Support Broker Verification List.

Questions worth asking

  • "What's your OPWDD authorization number, and is it current for this year?"
  • "Are you an independent broker, or do you work through an agency?" OPWDD allows both, and some brokers do both.
  • "How many families do you work with right now?"
  • "How will we meet, and how often?"

A good broker won't mind any of these. Our guide to choosing a Support Broker has more questions, and your first months with a broker explains what the work looks like.

One exception: unpaid brokers

Some family members act as unpaid brokers. They still have to complete the initial training, but they don't need to apply for authorization. More on that in Can a parent be a Support Broker?

Spectrum Arch works with OPWDD-authorized Support Brokers who serve Capital Region families through our organization. Join our list and we'll talk through your situation.

Source: OPWDD, Support Broker Authorization FAQ (July 10, 2026); OPWDD ADM #19-ADM-05R, Authorization Standards for Support Brokers.
