Support Broker, Care Manager, or Fiscal Intermediary? Who Does What

Self-Direction involves several people and organizations, and their roles are easy to mix up. Here's who does what.

Last reviewed October 8, 2026

RoleWhat they doAsk them about
The person and familyMake the decisions: goals, supports, staff, and how the budget is spent (with Budget Authority).—
Circle of SupportPeople who know the person well. Help plan, review the budget, and support decisions.Ideas, practical help, honest feedback
Care Manager (CCO)Writes and updates the Life Plan, coordinates all services, helps with enrollment and requesting Self-Direction.Eligibility, waiver enrollment, the Life Plan, other services
Support BrokerHelps design and manage Self-Direction: the Circle, the budget, hiring, amendments, and renewals.The self-direction budget, staffing, IDGS, changes during the year
Fiscal Intermediary (FI)Employer of record for self-hired staff: payroll, background checks, trainings, paying approved bills, tracking the budget.Paychecks, timesheets, staff clearance, budget balance
OPWDDDetermines eligibility, sets the Personal Resource Account, and reviews and approves budgets.Usually reached through your Care Manager or broker

An easy way to remember it

The Care Manager plans your overall services. The Support Broker designs your self-direction budget and helps you run it. The FI makes the money and paperwork work.

Where families get stuck

  • "Who do I call about a late paycheck?" The FI.
  • "Can we add a class to the budget?" Your Support Broker.
  • "Our Medicaid letter says coverage is ending." Your Care Manager, right away.
  • "We want a different Support Broker." Your Care Manager can help you switch.

More detail on each: Support Brokers, Fiscal Intermediaries, and choosing a Care Coordination Organization.

Sources: OPWDD, Self-Direction; OPWDD Self-Direction Guidance (March 2022); OPWDD, FI Checklist.
