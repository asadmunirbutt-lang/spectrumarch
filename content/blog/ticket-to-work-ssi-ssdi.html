Many people with disabilities want to work but worry, reasonably, about losing SSI, SSDI, or Medicaid if they do. Social Security's Ticket to Work program is designed to make trying work less risky.

Who can use it

Adults ages 18 through 64 who receive SSI or SSDI because of a disability. It's free and voluntary.

What you get

Ticket to Work connects you with approved providers, including Employment Networks and your state's vocational rehabilitation agency (in New York, ACCES-VR), that can offer:

  • Career planning and counseling
  • Job search and placement help
  • Training and support on the job
  • Ongoing support to keep the job

Protections while you try

  • People actively participating and making progress may be protected from Social Security's medical continuing disability reviews.
  • Many people keep Medicaid or Medicare while working. In New York, the Medicaid Buy-In for Working People with Disabilities can help people keep Medicaid at higher incomes.
  • Social Security has work incentive rules that let people earn some income before benefits change.

Get benefits advice first

How work affects SSI and SSDI is complicated, and it's different for each program. Before starting a job or increasing hours, talk to a benefits counselor. Work Incentives Planning and Assistance (WIPA) counselors give free advice and can be reached through the Ticket to Work Help Line.

How to start

Call the Ticket to Work Help Line at 1-866-968-7842 (TTY 1-866-833-2967) to confirm eligibility and find providers, or visit choosework.ssa.gov.

Sources: SSA, What is the Ticket to Work Program?; SSA, What You Need to Know About Ticket to Work.
