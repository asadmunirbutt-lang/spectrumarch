Once someone is eligible for OPWDD services, one of the first things they do is choose a Care Coordination Organization, or CCO. The CCO assigns a Care Manager, and that person becomes the family's main guide through the system for years.

It's easy to treat this as a form to fill in. It's worth a little more thought than that.

What a Care Manager does

  • Writes and updates the Life Plan, the person-centered plan that lists goals and services
  • Helps with applications and enrollment, including the HCBS Waiver
  • Connects you with providers and services, and follows up when something isn't working
  • Coordinates with doctors, schools, and other agencies
  • Helps you request Self-Direction, if that's your choice

A responsive Care Manager can save a family months. One who is hard to reach can cost them just as much.

Questions to ask a CCO

  • How many people does each Care Manager support?
  • How quickly do Care Managers usually return calls and emails?
  • How often will we meet, and can meetings happen at home?
  • Do you have Care Managers with experience supporting autistic adults?
  • How familiar are your staff with Self-Direction?
  • Can you help with transition planning before age 21?
  • If we're unhappy with our Care Manager, how do we ask for a different one?

Talk to other families

Ask parents in local support groups which CCO they use and what their experience has been. Experiences vary a lot from one Care Manager to another, even within the same organization, and other families are often the most honest source.

You can change

If the fit isn't right, you can ask for a different Care Manager or move to another CCO. Don't wait years before raising concerns. The Care Manager is meant to work for you.
