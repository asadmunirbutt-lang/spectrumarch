"Where will my son or daughter live?" is one of the biggest questions families face. Group homes are the option most people have heard of, but they aren't the only one, and for many people not the best fit.

Living at home with supports

Many adults live with family while using OPWDD services: day programs, community habilitation, respite, or Self-Direction. This works best with a plan for what happens when parents can no longer provide care. Start that plan early. Our post on writing a letter of intent is a good first step.

Your own apartment, with a housing subsidy

OPWDD can help people live as independently as possible in their own home or apartment, rented on the regular market or through Section 8 or public housing, with an OPWDD housing subsidy toward the cost. The person is generally expected to contribute about 30% of their income toward housing. Support comes from staff who visit, rather than live there.

Family Care

In Family Care, a person lives in another family's home, which is certified and overseen by OPWDD. The family provides a home and day-to-day support. It can suit people who want a family setting and don't need round-the-clock staff.

Self-Direction options

Self-Direction has created options that weren't possible before:

  • Live-in Caregiver: a caregiver lives in an extra bedroom rent-free in exchange for providing support. (They can't be related to the person.)
  • Paid Neighbor: for people who need less hands-on help, a neighbor is paid a stipend to be on call for specific supports.

Certified residences

Certified homes such as IRAs, also called group homes, provide staff support up to 24 hours a day. Availability is limited and waits can be long. Our post on questions to ask when visiting a residential home can help when you're comparing.

Where to start

Tell your Care Manager your housing goal, even if it's years away, and ask OPWDD's Front Door about housing supports in your region.
