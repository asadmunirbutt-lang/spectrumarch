Over the years, families meet a lot of providers: day programs, respite agencies, brokers, employment programs, and eventually, for some, residential homes. Each kind has its own specific questions, and we've written about several. But a core set of questions works almost everywhere. This is that list.

About the people

  • Who will actually be working with my family member? Can we meet them?
  • How long have your staff typically stayed? What is turnover like?
  • What training do staff receive, especially on autism and communication?
  • Who is my main contact, and how do I reach them?

About how you work

  • How do you get to know a new person: their routines, likes, and dislikes?
  • How do you handle sensory needs, anxiety, or changes in routine?
  • What do you do when someone is having a hard day?
  • How much choice do the people you support have in what they do?

About communication

  • How and how often will you update us?
  • How quickly will we hear about an incident or injury?
  • How do we raise a concern, and what happens after we do?

About the practical side

  • Is there a waitlist? How long is it really?
  • What happens when staff are out sick?
  • Is transportation included?
  • What paperwork do you need from us to get started?

The questions you ask yourself afterwards

  • Did they talk to my family member, or only to me?
  • Did the people we met seem to enjoy the work?
  • Did they answer directly, or reassure without answering?
  • Would my family member feel safe and respected here?

Trust your instincts on those last ones. You know your family member better than anyone else in the room.
