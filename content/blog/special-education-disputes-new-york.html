Disagreements with a school district are common, and they don't have to become a fight. But it helps to know your options before you need them.

Start informally

  • Put your concern in writing to the teacher, the CSE chairperson, or the special education director, and keep copies.
  • Ask for a CSE meeting to review the IEP. Parents can request one.
  • Read the Prior Written Notice. When a district proposes or refuses a change, it must explain why in writing. That notice often shows exactly where the disagreement is.

The three formal options

1. Mediation

Mediation is free, voluntary, and confidential. A trained, neutral mediator helps you and the district talk it through and reach a written agreement. Mediators don't take sides or make decisions. You can request mediation at any time, even if you've already filed something else.

2. State complaint

If you believe the district broke a special education rule, for example by missing a deadline or not delivering services in the IEP, you can send a written, signed complaint to the New York State Education Department. It must be about something that happened within the past year.

3. Impartial (due process) hearing

For disagreements about identification, evaluation, placement, or whether your child is getting an appropriate education, you can request an impartial hearing before an independent hearing officer. The district must tell you about mediation, give you the Procedural Safeguards Notice, and tell you about free or low-cost legal help in your area. Decisions can be appealed.

Practical advice

  • Keep a simple file: IEPs, evaluations, emails, notes from phone calls with dates.
  • Stay focused on what your child needs, not on who was right.
  • Get help early. Parent centers, advocates, and attorneys can explain your rights, and some help is free.

Sources: NYSED, Dispute Resolution Options; NYSED, Mediation; NYSED, Q&A on Impartial Due Process Hearings.
