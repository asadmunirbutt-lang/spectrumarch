Self-Direction has grown a lot in New York, and so have the frustrations: budgets that take months, paperwork that overwhelms families, and people who struggle to self-direct because they don't have family nearby. OPWDD has been studying how to fix that. Here's where things stand.

The review

In March 2024, OPWDD hired the consulting firm Guidehouse to assess its Self-Direction model. Guidehouse gathered feedback from more than 9,000 people: people who self-direct, families, Care Coordination Organizations, Fiscal Intermediaries, and Support Brokers. It also looked at how other states run similar programs.

The final report was released on October 30, 2025. It makes eight recommendations, supported by 32 activities.

What the recommendations focus on

OPWDD has described the recommendations as aimed at three things:

  • Improving the budgeting process, which is the part of Self-Direction families most often describe as slow and confusing.
  • Making Self-Direction available to more people.
  • Better supporting people who don't have a circle of support at home, or who have lost it, for example after a parent dies.

OPWDD calls the report "the first step toward an improved Self-Direction model." It hasn't published a timeline for putting the recommendations in place, so nothing changes for families yet.

What this means for you right now

  • The current rules still apply. Budgets, Fiscal Intermediaries, and Support Brokers work the way they do today. Our step-by-step guide reflects the current process.
  • Read the plain-language version. OPWDD published a Plain Language version of the report alongside the full one.
  • Your experience counts. Questions or feedback on Self-Direction policy can go to OPWDD at self.direction.redesign@opwdd.ny.gov.

We'll write about the changes in detail as OPWDD announces them.
