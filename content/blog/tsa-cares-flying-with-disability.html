Airport security is loud, crowded, unpredictable, and involves strangers and touch, which is hard for many people with disabilities. You can't skip security, but you can make it much easier.

What TSA Cares is

TSA Cares is the Transportation Security Administration's helpline for travelers with disabilities, medical conditions, and other special circumstances. You can ask questions about screening ahead of time, and request a Passenger Support Specialist: a TSA officer trained to help people through the checkpoint.

How to request help

  • Call 855-787-2227 or use the TSA Cares form on tsa.gov, about 72 hours before your flight.
  • Tell them the airport, flight time, and what the traveler needs: for example, extra time, a quieter screening, help with medical devices, or keeping a familiar person close.
  • At the airport, you can also ask any officer or supervisor for a Passenger Support Specialist.

Preparing the traveler

  • Show what will happen with photos or videos of the airport and checkpoint, and practice the steps at home.
  • Pack comfort items such as noise-reducing headphones, snacks, and a favorite object, and keep them in reach.
  • Carry a short information card explaining how the person communicates and what helps.
  • Allow plenty of extra time, so nobody is rushed.

Medications and equipment

Bring medications in your carry-on, with labels. Medical devices and supplies are generally allowed but must be screened, so tell the officer about them before screening starts.

Beyond security

Airlines provide their own disability assistance, such as wheelchair help, pre-boarding, and seating. Request it when you book and confirm it before you fly.

Sources: TSA, Assistance during screening; TSA, Request for TSA Cares Assistance; TSA, Screening travelers on the autism spectrum.
