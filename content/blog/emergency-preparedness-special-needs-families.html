Winter storms, ice, and long power outages are part of life in upstate New York. For families with a member who uses medical equipment, takes critical medications, or struggles with sudden change, a little planning makes an emergency far easier.

1. Write it down

Make a one-page emergency information sheet:

  • Name, date of birth, diagnoses, and allergies
  • Medications, doses, and the pharmacy
  • Doctors and specialists, with phone numbers
  • How the person communicates, and what helps them stay calm
  • Equipment they use, such as a wheelchair, feeding pump, oxygen, or communication device
  • Emergency contacts

Keep copies at home, in the car, in the go-bag, and on your phone.

2. Medications

  • Try to keep a few extra days of critical medications on hand, and ask your doctor and pharmacy how to do that.
  • Know which medications need refrigeration, and how you'd keep them cold in an outage.

3. Power

  • If someone depends on powered medical equipment, tell your utility company. Many utilities have a way to note customers with medical needs.
  • Keep backup batteries charged, and know how long each device runs without power.
  • Plan where you'd go if the power is out for days: a relative's home, a warming center, or a hotel.

4. A go-bag

Pack a bag you can grab in minutes: medications, the information sheet, chargers, copies of insurance and Medicaid cards, comfort items, headphones, familiar snacks, a change of clothes, and supplies for any equipment.

5. Prepare for the change itself

For many people with developmental disabilities, an evacuation or a dark, cold house is frightening mostly because it's unexpected. Talk about what might happen, practice leaving the house, and pack something familiar.

6. Sign up for alerts

Sign up for New York State's free emergency alerts (NY-Alert) and your county's emergency notifications.

Sources: Ready.gov, People with Disabilities; NY-Alert.
