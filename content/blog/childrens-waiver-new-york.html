Families of children with significant needs often hear that a "waiver" could help but aren't sure which one. For children in New York there are two main routes, and they can't be used at the same time.

The Children's Waiver

New York's Consolidated Children's Waiver provides Medicaid home and community-based services (HCBS) to children and youth under 21 who:

  • Have Medicaid, or are eligible for it
  • Meet the Children's Waiver level-of-care criteria and are at risk of needing institutional care, and
  • Fall into one of its target groups, which include children with serious emotional disturbance, children who are medically fragile, and children with developmental disabilities

It's run through the Department of Health and serves a broad group of children, not only those with developmental disabilities.

The OPWDD Comprehensive Waiver

Children with a qualifying developmental disability can instead enroll in OPWDD's HCBS Waiver, which has no upper age limit and continues into adulthood. It requires OPWDD eligibility, Medicaid, and the need for an ICF/IID level of care. See what the OPWDD HCBS Waiver is.

Only one at a time

A child is enrolled in one waiver or the other, not both. Which fits better depends on the child's primary needs, the services you're looking for, and planning for adulthood. A Care Manager can help you compare.

What about family income?

For children applying for waiver services, special rules can allow a child to qualify for Medicaid without counting all of the parents' income. OPWDD and the Department of Health publish guidance on the application process. Ask your Care Manager how the rules apply to your family before assuming your child won't qualify.

Where to start

If your child has a developmental disability, start with OPWDD eligibility and ask for a Care Manager. For other needs, ask your child's doctor or local Department of Social Services about the Children's Waiver and Health Home care management for children.
