Parents and caregivers of people with disabilities spend years learning systems, filling out forms, attending meetings, and providing care around the clock, often while working and raising other children. Exhaustion isn't a personal failing. It's a reason to get support.

Talk to someone who's been there

Parent to Parent of New York State is a statewide nonprofit that matches parents and caregivers one-to-one with a trained Support Parent who has a family member with a similar disability or health need. Matches are available for parents of young children and of adult sons and daughters, as well as grandparents, siblings, and foster or adoptive parents. Call 800-305-8817 or visit ptopnys.org.

Get practical help

  • OPWDD Family Support Services help families caring at home, with respite, recreation, reimbursement, counseling, and more.
  • Respite gives caregivers a regular break. Use it before you're at the breaking point.
  • Self-Direction can let you hire people you trust, so care doesn't rest on one person. See Support Brokerage.

Look after yourself

  • Keep your own health appointments. They're easy to put off for years.
  • Accept specific help. When someone offers, give them a concrete job: a meal, a school pickup, an hour of company.
  • Share the load. Bring siblings, relatives, and friends into a Circle of Support, so knowledge and responsibility aren't held by one person.
  • Watch for burnout: constant exhaustion, irritability, losing interest in things you used to enjoy. Talk to your doctor, and call or text 988 if you're in crisis.

Plan for the future

One of the biggest sources of worry is "what happens when I can't do this anymore?" Making a plan helps: a letter of intent, legal and financial planning, and a long-term support plan. Start with our posts on the letter of intent and supplemental needs trusts.
