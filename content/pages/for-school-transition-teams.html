Why timing matters

Adult services don't start automatically when school ends. To receive OPWDD services, a person must be found eligible, which requires showing a developmental disability that began before age 22, continues, and substantially limits daily functioning. Then they must enroll in Medicaid and the HCBS Waiver, choose a Care Manager, and build a plan. Each step takes time. Families who start in the last months of school often face a gap with no supports at all.

Records schools often already have

OPWDD's eligibility application is assembled by the family's Care Coordination Organization. It requires documents such as:

  • Cognitive (psychological) testing
  • Adaptive behavior assessments
  • Medical documentation and a recent physical or medical summary
  • A social or developmental history showing the disability began before age 22
  • A recent social evaluation

OPWDD also lists the IEP, 504 Plan, and other educational records as helpful. A current psychological evaluation with standardized adaptive scores can save a family months. If your district's last evaluation is several years old, consider whether a re-evaluation before graduation would serve the student. Our guide to OPWDD eligibility explains what reviewers look for.

What you can do

  • Raise OPWDD eligibility at CSE meetings starting in the early transition years, not just the final year.
  • Give families copies of evaluations and IEPs, and explain which ones matter for OPWDD.
  • Point families to the OPWDD Front Door to begin. In the Capital District, the number is 518-388-0398.
  • Mention ACCES-VR for students interested in work; vocational rehabilitation has its own transition services.
  • Make sure families know about SSI at 18, which is often the route to adult Medicaid.

Where Support Brokerage fits

Many young adults leaving school want a life built around their own interests rather than a single program, and OPWDD Self-Direction makes that possible. A Support Broker helps the family build a Circle of Support, turn the person's goals into a budget, and hire staff. For families coming out of school, planning for Self-Direction can start once eligibility is established, so the plan is ready when school ends.

Spectrum Arch connects Capital Region families with OPWDD-authorized Support Brokers. With the family's permission, you can refer them through our waitlist form, choosing "A Care Manager or other professional", or have them contact us directly. Families can also talk with us for free while they're still working on eligibility.
