ACCES-VR
Adult Career and Continuing Education Services - Vocational Rehabilitation, the New York State agency that helps people with disabilities prepare for, find, and keep jobs. It also serves students in transition from school.
Budget Authority
The part of Self-Direction where the person, with their Circle of Support, decides how their Self-Direction budget is spent within OPWDD's rules. People who self-direct with Budget Authority work with a Support Broker. See Employer vs. Budget Authority.
CANS-NY
Child and Adolescent Needs and Strengths, the assessment OPWDD uses for children. Adults are assessed with the CAS.
Care Manager
The person, employed by a Care Coordination Organization, who writes and updates the Life Plan, helps the person get and keep services and benefits such as Medicaid, and is the main point of contact with OPWDD. Also called a Care Coordinator.
CAS (Coordinated Assessment System)
The assessment OPWDD uses for everyone 18 or older who receives or applies for its services. It identifies strengths, needs, and interests, and is usually repeated every two years. See our CAS guide.
CCO (Care Coordination Organization)
An organization that provides OPWDD care management. The Front Door connects new applicants with a CCO, which assembles the eligibility application. See choosing a CCO.
CHOICES
OPWDD's electronic record system. Eligibility documents are uploaded to CHOICES, and the CAS Summary is added there.
Circle of Support
The people who help a person who self-directs plan and make decisions: family, friends, and others who know them well. The Support Broker helps build and run it. See building a Circle of Support.
Community Habilitation
An HCBS Waiver service where staff help a person build skills and take part in community life, at home or out in the community.
DDRO / Regional Field Office
OPWDD's regional offices. They run the Front Door, determine eligibility, and oversee services in their area. The Capital District's offices are in Schenectady.
Developmental disability (in New York law)
Defined in Mental Hygiene Law § 1.03(22): a disability that began before age 22, has continued or is expected to continue indefinitely, and substantially limits a person's ability to function in society. See OPWDD eligibility.
Employer Authority
The part of Self-Direction where the person or their family recruits, hires, trains, schedules, and supervises their own staff. The Fiscal Intermediary is the employer on paper. See hiring your own staff.
FI (Fiscal Intermediary)
The OPWDD-approved organization that handles payroll for self-hired staff, pays approved expenses, and tracks the Self-Direction budget. See Fiscal Intermediaries.
Front Door
OPWDD's starting point for anyone seeking services: eligibility, information about options, and a connection to a CCO. Capital District Front Door: 518-388-0398.
HCBS Waiver
OPWDD's Comprehensive Home and Community-Based Services 1915(c) Waiver, the Medicaid program that pays for most OPWDD community services, including Self-Direction. Enrollment requires Medicaid. See what the HCBS Waiver is.
IDGS (Individual Directed Goods and Services)
A Self-Direction budget category for goods, services, and supports that aren't otherwise available and that meet a goal in the person's plan. See IDGS.
LCED (Level of Care Eligibility Determination)
The determination that a person needs the level of care an ICF/IID would provide. It's required for HCBS Waiver services and is redetermined every year.
Life Plan
The person-centered plan, written by the Care Manager, that describes the person's goals and the services and supports that help them reach those goals.
Medicaid Buy-In for Working People with Disabilities
A New York Medicaid program that lets people with disabilities ages 16 to 64 who are working keep Medicaid with higher income and savings limits. See Medicaid and OPWDD services.
PRA (Personal Resource Account)
The yearly amount available for a person's Self-Direction budget, based on their assessed needs. Spending can't exceed it, and it resets each budget year. See the Self-Direction budget.
QIDP
Qualified Intellectual Disabilities Professional. In certain certified residential settings, the QIDP plays the planning role a Care Manager plays elsewhere.
Respite
Short-term care that gives unpaid family caregivers a break. See respite options.
Self-Direction
The OPWDD option that lets a person choose and manage their own supports, staff, and schedule, instead of receiving services only through a provider agency. See our step-by-step guide.
Significant Change in Condition
A major change in a person's health, abilities, or behavior. When a Care Manager determines one has happened, the person is referred for reassessment before their regular two-year CAS.
Support Broker
An OPWDD-authorized professional who helps a person who self-directs with Budget Authority build a Circle of Support and create and manage a Self-Direction budget. Brokers are reauthorized every year by July 31. See what a Support Broker does.
Waiver of parental deeming
For children under 18 whose family income is too high for Medicaid, a way to have parental income and resources not counted, so the child can qualify for Medicaid and HCBS Waiver services.

Sources: OPWDD ADM 23-ADM-06R (revised July 2025); OPWDD, Coordinated Assessment System; OPWDD, Support Broker Authorization FAQ (July 2026); OPWDD Self-Direction Guidance.
