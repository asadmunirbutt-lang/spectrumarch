What a Support Broker does

Support Brokers help people with developmental disabilities who self-direct their OPWDD services with Budget Authority. The broker helps build a Circle of Support, turns the person's goals into a Self-Direction budget, and helps manage that budget and plan over time. It's skilled, personal work, and there's real demand for it in our region. Read more about the role.

Step 1: Express interest

Fill out OPWDD's Support Broker interest form (linked from the authorization FAQ below). OPWDD then emails the next steps.

Step 2: Complete the eight required trainings, in order

  1. Three prerequisites (prerecorded)

    Introduction to Self-Direction; Self-Advocacy/Self-Determination; and Introduction and Advanced Person-Centered Planning. They're available on demand on OPWDD's Statewide Learning Management System (SLMS). Each ends with a ten-question quiz; you need 70% to pass. If you completed the last two through an HCBS provider or CCO employer, you may be able to get credit.

  2. A Welcome Call

    A required one-hour virtual meeting with OPWDD Central Office staff covering expectations, professionalism, and ethics.

  3. Broker Training Institute, Modules 1 to 4 (live)

    Four live Webex classes taken in sequence: the philosophy and history of Self-Direction, broker responsibilities, billing and documentation, and Circle of Support training.

  4. Developing a Self-Direction Budget (live)

    How to build budgets for people who self-direct. You'll need the desktop version of Microsoft Excel; the free web version doesn't have the features the budget template needs.

The live classes each end with an open-book quiz due by 9 am the next morning. Missing a class that's a prerequisite for the next one drops you from the rest of the series until the next available class.

Step 3: Six read-and-sign trainings

After the eight courses, you review and attest to six more: Privacy and Security of Health Information, Overview of Developmental Disabilities, Medicaid Compliance, PICA, PRAISE (incident and abuse reporting), and Rights and Responsibilities of Persons Receiving Services.

Step 4: Apply for authorization

Submit the Support Broker Authorization form, with your training certificates, to OPWDD as a digitally signed PDF. OPWDD processes accepted forms every two weeks and emails an approval letter with your authorization number. Background screening requirements also apply.

Step 5: Stay authorized

Authorization runs until July 31 each year. To continue, you reauthorize annually, attest to three annual trainings, and document 12 hours of professional development (prorated in your first year). OPWDD's quarterly Fiscal Intermediary/Support Broker conferences count toward those hours.

Ways to work

  • Independent broker: you contract directly with families. You can choose to be on OPWDD's public list of brokers.
  • Agency broker: you work for an OPWDD-authorized HCBS provider that offers Support Brokerage.
  • Both, where the agency allows it.
