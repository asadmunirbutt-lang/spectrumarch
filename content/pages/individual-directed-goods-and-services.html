What IDGS is

Individual Directed Goods and Services (IDGS) are services, equipment, or supplies that aren't otherwise provided through the OPWDD waiver or the Medicaid State Plan, and that address a need identified in the person's plan. IDGS is available to people who self-direct with Budget Authority.

The rules an IDGS purchase has to meet

Under OPWDD guidance, an IDGS item has to:

  • Connect to a need or goal in the person's Life Plan
  • Increase independence, or substitute for human assistance that would otherwise be paid for
  • Support community living and inclusion

It also can't be something available through the Medicaid State Plan. If Medicaid already covers it, the self-direction budget can't buy it.

What that can look like

Whether a specific item is approved depends on the person's plan and OPWDD's review, but IDGS is commonly used for things like a community class tied to a social or skills goal, a membership that supports health and community participation, or supplies that help someone do more on their own. The key is the "why": how this item moves a goal forward.

Making a strong case

  • Start from the goal, not the item. "Build friendships outside the family" leads naturally to a weekly class.
  • Explain the link in plain words: what will change for the person.
  • Show it's not covered elsewhere.
  • Review it. At Circle of Support meetings, check whether it's actually helping.

A Support Broker builds IDGS into the budget with you and documents the reasoning. See the Self-Direction budget and PRA for how it fits with the rest of the budget.

Sources: OPWDD Self-Direction Guidance (March 2022); OPWDD, Self-Direction.
